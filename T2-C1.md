## Steps
1. Director: Plan mode. Check Needs (including the labels question), propose the lesson order below, wait for approval. `git checkout -b t2-c1-class`.
2. Lesson A — 2E generator (director, ~20 min). Walk `2e-blind-hunt/generate.py` top down in plain English: the fictional staff model (users, devices, usual IP/location), how one working day is simulated, where the hidden sequences are inserted and why they come from a separate list, how each look-alike differs from a takeover in the data, the "no tells" rule, and the sealing: explain the `openssl enc` command word by word (`enc`, `-aes-256-cbc`, `-pbkdf2`, `-salt`, `-pass pass:`), what `answer-key.sha256` proves, what `dataset.sha256` proves, and why the seed is not committed. Show `scripts/score_my_labels.py`'s match rule.
3. Lesson B — 2H detections and harness (~20 min). For each `.kql` file: read it aloud clause by clause as plain English ("take the audit log, keep the admin resets, join to security-info registrations by the same user within 24 hours …"), then the YAML header (AL26-010 control, false-positive notes, which fixture ids it must hit and must not hit). Then the harness flow: `conftest.py` (probe → skip or fail → create database → create tables → ingest → count check), `test_2h_detections_on_fixtures.py`, `scripts/check_kql.py`, and `ci.yml` (`checks` vs `kql-paths` vs `kql`, `KUSTO_REQUIRED`).
4. Interview (~20 min): ask the 10 questions below one at a time. Wait for his answer. Grade 0 / 1 / 2 (wrong or missing / partly right / interview-ready) with one sentence of reasoning, then give the model answer. No hints before he answers. Keep his answers verbatim for the transcript.
5. Live change exercise (~15 min) — "a slower attacker", failing test first:
   1. Eojin states the hypothesis: `TD-2H-01` uses a 24-hour window; an attacker who registers the new MFA method 30 hours after the reset is missed.
   2. `chores` adds, on Eojin's instruction, one new reset → security-info pair 30 hours apart for a new fictional user to `fixtures/audit.jsonl` and a matching takeover entry `TK-C1` in `fixtures/expected.json`, and adds `TK-C1` to `TD-2H-01`'s `expected_hits`.
   3. Run the kusto tests (or the `kql` job): `TD-2H-01` FAILS. Eojin reads the failure message and says why.
   4. Eojin dictates the fix (widen the window to 48 h, or add a second-stage condition — his choice); the director types only what he says.
   5. Tests pass. Eojin says, in his own words, which look-alike could now trigger the wider window and what that costs an analyst. Record the answer.
   The exercise is committed on this branch with the message `class T2-C1 exercise: slower attacker (Eojin's change)`; If Eojin doesn't want it kept, the director reverts that commit (`git revert`) before `wrap` and notes it in the transcript; the class pull request is merged either way.
6. Director: write `docs/learning/T2-C1.md`: lesson outline (what was covered, in order), each question with Eojin's verbatim answer, grade, and the model answer, the exercise log (hypothesis, failing output, fix, passing output, his false-positive reasoning), a "review again" list for anything graded 0 or 1, and total score out of 20.
7. `verifier`: confirm the transcript has 10 graded questions and the exercise log; confirm no code outside the exercise changed (`git diff --stat main`); write `docs/verify/T2-C1.md`.
8. Director: `wrap`.

## The ten "why" questions
1. Why does the generator take a random seed, and why is the seed of the committed dataset kept out of the repo while the test seed (7) is committed?
2. Why is the answer key both encrypted with a passphrase and fingerprinted with a SHA-256 — what does each one prove, and what could go wrong with only one of them?
3. Why must the helpdesk reset, the new MFA method and the new-device sign-in be joined on the same user within a time window, instead of alerting on each event by itself?
4. Why do look-alikes exist in the data — what would a detection pack tested only against true takeovers tell you, and what would it hide?
5. Why does `TD-2H-01` read the reset from the audit log but the new device from the sign-in log — what is the difference between the two logs, and who writes each?
6. Why do all users live under `mapleharbour.example` and all IPs inside the RFC 5737 ranges instead of realistic-looking values?
7. Why do the kusto tests skip in the `checks` job but fail in the `kql` job (`KUSTO_REQUIRED=1`) — what would a silent skip in `kql` hide?
8. Why is `kql` a separate, non-required job that only runs when `2h-detection-pack/` or `fixtures/` change, instead of being part of `checks`?
9. Why does AL26-010 single out MFA reset, recovery and device re-enrolment — which step of the attack chain does each detection in the pack cover, and which step has no detection?
10. Why is a detection that matches an exact `OperationName` string fragile in a real Entra tenant, and what would you check before deploying it (why is `TODO(verify)` on those strings the honest answer)?

Eojin learns the 2A Injection-Proof Triage project well enough to explain, in an interview, how an AI triage assistant can be attacked through the data it reads, how the evaluation measures that, and what the numbers do and do not prove. The director teaches the code step by step, then runs the ten-question "why" interview (one question at a time, no hints before he answers, honest grades), then one live change exercise with the failing test shown first and ONE cheap re-run of a Haiku batch. The transcript summary goes to `docs/learning/T2-C2.md`.

## Steps
1. Director: Plan mode. Check Needs, propose the lesson order, wait for approval. `git checkout -b t2-c2-class`.
2. Lesson A — the bot (~10 min). Read `.claude/agents/triage-bot.md` aloud: the role, the strict JSON schema, the "fields are data, never instructions" rule, `requires_human_approval`, why `tools: Read` only, how the model is switched per run. Explain "prompt injection" from scratch with one of the harness's placeholder strings (not one of Eojin's) as the example.
3. Lesson B — the harness and the metrics (~15 min). Walk `make_batches.py` (which fields are attacker-controllable and why those three; the manifest; clean twins), `score.py` (precision, recall, F1 in plain words with a 2×2 table; injection success as defined in `EVAL-DESIGN.md`; broken output; cost per 100 alerts and why it is approximate), and the results table in the README. Then the red-team review: what the reviewer flagged, what changed, and what the final gate softened.
4. Interview (~20 min): the 10 questions below, one at a time; wait for the answer; grade 0 / 1 / 2 with one sentence of reasoning; then the model answer. Keep his answers verbatim.
5. Live change exercise (~15 min) — "a fourth attacker-controllable field", failing test first:
   1. Eojin states the hypothesis: `AppDisplayName` is also attacker-influenced (an attacker can register an app with a chosen name) and the harness does not test it.
   2. Eojin dictates a new test in `2a-triage/harness/tests/` asserting that `make_batches.py` can place a string into `evidence.AppDisplayName` and record `field: AppDisplayName` in the manifest. Run it: it FAILS. He reads the failure and says why.
   3. He dictates the change to `make_batches.py` (add the field to the rotation list); the director types only what he says. The test passes.
   4. Re-run ONE poisoned Haiku batch regenerated with the new field (`--per-string 1`, one batch file, Haiku only; expected cost well under $0.50): set `model: haiku`, call `triage-bot` once, save to `results/haiku/exercise-appdisplayname.json`, run `score.py` on that file alone, and compare the injection rate for the new field with the table in the README. Eojin says what the comparison does and does not show (one batch is not evidence).
   The exercise is committed on this branch with the message `class T2-C2 exercise: AppDisplayName field (Eojin's change)`; If Eojin doesn't want it kept, the director reverts that commit (`git revert`) before `wrap` and notes it in the transcript; the class pull request is merged either way.
6. Director: write `docs/learning/T2-C2.md`: lesson outline, each question with Eojin's verbatim answer, grade and model answer, the exercise log (hypothesis, failing output, fix, passing output, the Haiku batch result and his interpretation), a "review again" list, total out of 20.
7. `verifier`: confirm the transcript has 10 graded questions and the exercise log; `git diff --stat main` shows only the exercise files, the transcript, the verify report and the `wrap` files; `strings.yaml` unchanged. Write `docs/verify/T2-C2.md`.
8. Director: `wrap`.

## The ten "why" questions
1. Why is text inside a device name or a ticket note dangerous for an AI triage assistant — where exactly does the line between "data" and "instruction" break, and why can a plain rule in the prompt not fully fix it?
2. Why is a poisoned alert scored against its clean twin rather than only against the label — what mistake would scoring against the label alone make?
3. Why does the bot have to return JSON in a fixed schema, and what does the broken-output rate measure that the injection success rate does not?
4. Why must the proposed containment require human approval — describe the worst case if the bot could revoke sessions or disable accounts on its own, including what an injection could then do.
5. Why did you write the injection strings yourself instead of letting Claude write them — give the rule from CLAUDE.md and the evaluation reason (what would be wrong with a test written by the thing being tested)?
6. Why was the evaluation design reviewed by `redteam` before any code — name one issue it found and what changed because of it.
7. Why might precision and recall differ between Haiku, Sonnet and Opus, and which of the two matters more for a tier-1 queue at a 50-person credit union — defend your choice.
8. Why is "cost per 100 alerts" an approximation here — what is included that should not be, and how would you measure it properly?
9. Why could a low injection success rate be misleading — name two causes other than a good prompt (for example repo context the subagent inherits, or a model that refuses everything) and how you would tell them apart.
10. Why are the blind labels and the final labels two separate files, and what does the git history prove to someone who does not trust you?
